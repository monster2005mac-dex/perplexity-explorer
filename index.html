📖  The Mathematics of Perplexity

Perplexity (PPL) is the standard NLP metric for how well a probability model predicts a sample sequence.

PPL(W) = N√(∏ᵢ₌₁ᴺ 1/P(wᵢ|w<ᵢ)) = eℒ = 2ℒbits

1. As the Geometric Mean of Inverses

Each token has a conditional probability P(wᵢ|w₁…wᵢ₋₁). Perplexity is the geometric mean of all 1/P values — equivalently, the branching factor the model faced on average.

2. Relationship to Cross-Entropy Loss

The two forms are mathematically identical:

ℒ = −1/N Σ ln P(wᵢ|w<ᵢ)   ⟹   PPL = eℒ
Proof: (∏ 1/P)1/N = exp(1/N · Σ ln(1/P)) = exp(1/N · Σ −lnP) = exp(ℒ)

3. Five Equivalent Forms

Canonical (log-space, numerically safe)PPL = exp( −(1/N) Σᵢ log P(wᵢ|w<ᵢ) )
Geometric mean of inverses (image formula)PPL = ( ∏ᵢ 1/P(wᵢ|w<ᵢ) )^(1/N)
Exponentiated cross-entropyPPL = e^ℒ    where ℒ = mean −ln P
Base-2 versionPPL = 2^H    where H = mean −log₂P (bits/token)
Inverse joint probabilityPPL = P(w₁,…,wₙ)^(−1/N)

4. The "Effective Vocabulary" Intuition

PPL = K means the model was, on average, as confused as picking uniformly from K words at each step.

  • PPL = 1: Perfect certainty — predicted every token with 100% probability.
  • PPL 2–10: Very predictable text (repetitive, formulaic).
  • PPL 10–30: Excellent — modern LLMs on natural English text.
  • PPL 30–200: Poor — unusual wording or domain shift.
  • PPL = vocab_size: Random guessing (distilgpt2 vocab: 50,257).

5. Information-Theoretic Connection

Cross-entropy H = ln(PPL) is the average number of nats needed to encode each token. In bits: log₂(PPL). A lower-PPL model achieves better compression.
